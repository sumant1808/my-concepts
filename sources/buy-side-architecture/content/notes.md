# UK asset manager functional architecture: notes

## Layer logic
- Six layers in a stack. Top to bottom: L1 Clients and Distribution, L2 Investment Management, L3 Trading and Execution, L4 Investment Operations and Accounting, L5 Product, Risk, Compliance and Regulatory, L6 Platform (data, integration, controls). It has 35 groups, 90 components, 19 external parties and 32 key flows in three stories (life of a listed trade, a fund deal and a capital call).
- L1 to L4 follow the life of money and decisions: win and serve the client, decide, execute, then record, value and administer. L5 is the oversight layer: it watches L1 to L4 and produces regulatory output. L6 is shared by every layer.
- There is one architecture, tagged by segment: A = Active, P = Passive/ETF, L = Alternatives. Components that only one segment needs sit in their own groups so they can be shaded on the diagram. P groups are Index and Replication and ETF Primary Market. L groups are Deal Management, Portfolio Monitoring, Private Markets Transaction Execution, Private Fund Administration, LP pipeline, portal and reporting. A-only components are Research and the Optimiser. OMS/EMS and TCA/best execution are tagged A,P because index trading uses them too.
- Every component is a system view: what it does plus its role (system of record, engagement, calculation, data, integration or control). It is not a value-chain activity. Each value-chain block was checked against the components; the Exit and Realisation gap found in review is closed by C86. There is no mapping file.
- external=true marks components a third party usually runs (administrator, TA, iNAV agent, proxy adviser, LP portal). The manager still owns oversight of them through L5 Delegate Oversight (C80-C82): NAV and administrator oversight, depositary and trustee liaison, and third-party oversight. Box management (C83) and client money (C84) stay with the manager.

## Where BIAN fits and where it does not
- Source: the BIAN Service Landscape v10 value chain view (bian.org). It is the only version I checked, and later releases may rename domains. 37 of the 90 components carry a BIAN name, and every name is copied as seen from the v10 view. C49 carries both fund-type domains (Mutual Fund Administration; Unit Trust Administration) because each BIAN domain covers the administration of its fund type, so splitting them across NAV and TA would imply a split BIAN does not make.
- It fits well for listed post-trade and reference data: Market Order, Market Order Execution, Trade Confirmation Matching, Trade Settlement, Corporate Action, Financial Instrument Valuation, Securities Position Keeping, Account Reconciliation, Stock Lending and Repos, Collateral Allocation Management, Financial Instrument Reference Data Management, Legal Entity Directory and Market Information Management.
- It partly fits investment management and funds: Investment Portfolio Management, Investment Portfolio Analysis, Financial Market Research, Mutual Fund Administration, Unit Trust Administration, Guideline Compliance, Regulatory Reporting, Market Risk and Counterparty Risk. These domains assume a bank serving customers, so treat them as closest fit, not as the same thing.
- It fits weakly but is used: Customer Relationship Management, Lead and Opportunity Management, Party Lifecycle Management, Customer Agreement, Customer Case Management, Product Design, Product Directory, Corporate Loan (lender side, used for private credit), Financial Accounting (the manager's own ledger, C55), Party Authentication, Archive Services and Contractor and Supplier Agreement. Trade and Price Reporting was tried for transaction reporting (C66) and dropped, because it reads as market trade and price publication; C66 uses Regulatory Reporting instead.
- I saw no BIAN domain for any of these, so the field is left empty: index data and replication, ETF creation/redemption and iNAV, tracking error, private-markets deal pipeline, due diligence, investment committee, portfolio company monitoring, real assets, capital calls and distributions, LP register, fees and carried interest, partnership accounting, stewardship and proxy voting, TCA/best execution, market abuse surveillance, financial promotions, SM&CR, KYC/screening, value assessment, operational resilience, NAV and depositary oversight, box management, client money, derivatives and SFT reporting, exits, fund-of-funds dealing, restricted lists and ETF listing. BIAN's "Investor Relations" is about the bank's own shareholders, so I did not use it for LPs.

## Other inputs (why each is used)
- **FINOS CALM 1.1:** uses CALM 1.1 node-type and relationship vocabulary for the legend. This is not a CALM document, and flows and controls are not modelled. Every component's node-type comes from the schema enum: actor, ecosystem, system, service, database, network, ldap, webclient, data-asset. FIX and Swift are message standards, not CALM protocols.
- **ISO 20022 and ISO 15022 over Swift:** the custodian, depositary and TA interfaces. The business areas are verified: setr (Securities Trade), sese (Settlement), semt (Securities Management), seev (Securities Events), colr (Collateral), reda (Reference Data), auth (Authorities). The secl area is not used by any component, so it is not listed. A Swift document also maps MT 540-543 to sese/semt/seev equivalents.
- **FIX:** the manager-broker-venue protocol for orders, executions and allocations, including pre-trade allocation. The FIX Trading Community maintains it.
- **Common Domain Model (FINOS with ISDA, ICMA, ISLA):** optional. Use it only for the OTC derivatives collateral and securities lending flows.
- **ILPA templates:** the alternatives LP reporting standard: Reporting Template v2.0 (fees, expenses, carry), the Performance Templates, and the Capital Call and Distribution Template.
- **LEI (ISO 17442), ISIN (ISO 6166) and FIGI (OMG):** the identifiers for the party and security masters. GLEIF and ANNA publish an ISIN-to-LEI mapping. FIGI also covers loans and other assets that have no ISIN.
- **FDC3 (FINOS):** desktop interoperability across front-office apps, using intents, context data and an app directory.
- **ArchiMate (The Open Group):** an optional notation. Components map to ArchiMate application components, services and data objects.
- **UK rules verified in this run:** SDR labels and the anti-greenwashing rule (FCA), the CCI regime under PS25/20 (rules from 8 June 2027) and the UK Stewardship Code 2026 (FRC, effective 1 January 2026).

## Limitations
- UK rules are named as in the FCA Handbook. Those marked not verified were not checked against the source for this draft: Consumer Duty, SM&CR, UK UCITS/COLL, the UK AIFM regime, UK MiFIR transaction reporting, FATCA/CRS, the FCA operational resilience rules, CASS, UK EMIR and SFTR, FinDatEx templates and GIPS. Validate before external use.
- The ISO 20022 message-level mappings are analyst knowledge built on the verified business areas: setr for fund orders, seev for proxy voting, camt for cash statements, acmt for fund accounts (now on C50). MiFIR reports use ISO 20022 auth (C66); FCA returns and AIFMD Annex IV use XML schemas (C67, to confirm).
- BIAN was checked against v10 only. It is bank-oriented and has no buy-side or private-markets coverage, so the layering is our own and BIAN names are labels, not structure.
- The 32 links are illustrative. The real integration estate is far denser, and most components read from L6.
- This is an archetype, not a client's estate. It is AI-reviewed but not practitioner-validated. It assumes the manager is both UK UCITS manager and AIFM, and it leaves out hedge funds, synthetic ETFs and advice. There are no agentic AI, vendor names or statistics.
- IBOR (C17) covers Active and Passive positions. Alternatives positions are held in C24-C26 and C51, and a total-portfolio view comes from the enterprise data platform (C72).
- The FDC3 source URL (fdc3-new.finos.org) was not changed or re-checked in this pass.

Act II

A seven-layer
stack, not a chatbot.

Agentic AI at this scale needs deliberate architecture — governance is not a bottom layer, it spans every layer above.

The stack

1 · Experience & InteractionChat copilots, proactive alerts, client-facing report agents, RFP drafting UIs.
2 · Agent & OrchestrationPlanner-executor / supervisor-worker patterns coordinating narrow, specialized agents.
3 · Reasoning & ModelLLMs for synthesis plus quant/financial models — factor, risk, optimization, transcript analysis.
4 · Memory & ContextMandate/IPS history, prior rationale, client interaction history, full audit trail.
5 · Tooling & IntegrationConnectors into OMS/EMS, custodians, market data, portfolio accounting.
6 · Data & Market ContextPositions, licensed market data, alt-data, filings, entitlement/MNPI controls.
7 · Governance & GuardrailsBounded autonomy, RBAC, mandatory sign-off gates, kill-switch, observability — spans all six layers above.

Autonomy is not uniform across the stack

The same seven layers support very different autonomy levels depending on the use case — reconciliation runs with high autonomy and typed, validated plans; anything client-facing or advice-adjacent stays assistive, with a human approving before anything reaches a client or the market.

Assistive
Research, surveillance, client reporting — agent drafts, human approves
Recommend
Portfolio drift monitoring — agent proposes, PM signs off
Bounded autonomy
Trade execution — parameter-limited, anomalies routed to human
High autonomy
Reconciliation — typed/validated plans, exceptions escalated

Act III

Grounded,
not hypothetical.

SEC, FCA, and ESMA are already applying existing rules to agentic AI — none has purpose-built AI rules yet, which is itself the risk to manage.

Three regimes, one common thread: outcomes, not methods

SEC · Investment Advisers Act

No AI-specific rule. Existing fiduciary duty (care + loyalty) and Rule 206(4)-7 compliance program apply directly — 2026 exam priorities add AI oversight and vendor-selection documentation.

FCA · COBS + SM&CR

Suitability and best-execution attach to the outcome, not the method — though COBS suitability duties are narrower for professional/eligible-counterparty clients than retail, which covers most institutional long-only mandates. A named Senior Manager must be accountable for AI-driven outcomes under SM&CR regardless of client classification.

ESMA · MiFID II + AIFMD

May 2024 statement: firms must meet MiFID II conduct obligations when using AI, and watch for algorithmic bias, opacity, and overreliance. AIFMD delegation oversight extends to AI components performing portfolio/risk functions.

UK · CCI Regime (PS25/20)

Final rules published Dec 2025, replacing PRIIPs KID. Any agent drafting retail-facing product information — fact sheets, RFP responses — must source cost/risk figures from a compliant calculation engine, not free-text generation.

Honest caveat: all three regimes are stretching existing frameworks over agentic systems — none has settled guidance for autonomous, multi-step agents specifically. Duty-of-care and best-execution tests were written for single decisions, not agent chains. Treat this as a live interpretive area to revisit with counsel as guidance develops, not a closed compliance checklist.

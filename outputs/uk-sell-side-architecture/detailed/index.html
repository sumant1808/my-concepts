Gaps: where no standard fits

  • No open standard for KYC due-diligence data or onboarding workflow
  • No BIAN domain for KYC screening, trade booking/enrichment, front-to-back reconciliation, control room, structured products lifecycle, single-dealer platform, surveillance, best execution, client clearing, compression, locates, TRS, rehypothecation, client assets, data lineage or valuation adjustments

  • No common standard for tax reclaim submissions
  • Operational resilience, third-party oversight and conduct have regulation but no data or messaging standard
  • ISO 20022 for sell-side execution (setr) is little used; FIX dominates
  • FpML/CDM cover OTC derivatives, repo, lending and collateral, not cash equity or bond settlement

Limitations and assumptions

What this diagram does not establish, and the choices made to draw one archetype.

Limitations

  • CALM is vocabulary only. Every component carries a CALM 1.1 node type as a label, but this is not a CALM document: no relationships, flows or controls are modelled.
  • BIAN names are closest-fit labels, checked against the version 10 service landscape only. BIAN is oriented to banks and has no lifecycle view of capital markets, so the structure is the analyst's own. A card with no BIAN line had no matching domain. Later releases may rename domains.
  • ISO 20022 is shown at business area level only (for example secl, sese, colr, auth). Message identifiers were not checked, and sell-side use of setr and fxtr was not checked.
  • Swift MT numbers are analyst knowledge and are not verified. Verified: the coexistence of ISO 15022 and ISO 20022 for securities (MT to ISO 20022 is coexistence, not a forced cut-over), and the end of MT/MX coexistence for cross-border payments on 22 November 2025 (MT103/202 retired).
  • 19 of 41 standards entries are marked not verified. They were not checked against the source for this draft and should be validated before external use. They include UK MiFIR, best execution, pre- and post-trade transparency, EMIR risk-mitigation and clearing duties, CFTC Part 43, SEC SBSR, UMR phase-in and CCP margin models, CASS and 15c3-3, Reg SHO, 15c3-5 and RTS 6, KYC and AML, MiFID categorisation and suitability, ISDA, GMRA and GMSLA, LEI, ISIN, CFI and FIGI, FRTB, SA-CCR and BCBS 239, ISDA DRR, US broker-dealer regimes, MiFID II recording of communications, UK MAR Articles 11 and 18, and the Swift MT numbers. Mapping to components is indicative, not legal or regulatory advice.
  • FIX names were checked against a third-party FIX Latest dictionary. CDM was checked at model level, not function level. FpML and CDM references are family level.
  • The key flows are illustrative; the real integration estate is far denser and most components also read from the platform.
  • This is an archetype, not a client estate. Office assignment is a convention and banks differ. Status: AI-reviewed, not practitioner-validated. It has no vendor names, no statistics and no agentic AI layer.

Assumptions

  1. Archetype: a global bank's markets division (sales and trading only) with UK, EU and US legal entities, dealing as principal and agent across EQ, FI, FX and DR.
  2. Out of scope: primary issuance (ECM/DCM), research production, corporate and retail banking, treasury ALM beyond trading funding, and agentic AI.
  3. The bank runs its own custody business (S7), so custody components are internal; CSDs, ICSDs, CCPs, CLS, agent banks, trade repositories, triparty agents, data vendors and the messaging network are third-party run and appear as external parties. Four FMI touchpoints (C28, C41, C49, C50) are drawn in-stage to show the flow; they are the same parties as X13, X04, X05 and X07.
  4. Stages follow the owner's 10-stage Trade Flow Value Chain. A trade does not pass every stage: FX skips custody, OTC uncleared skips CCP clearing, listed derivatives skip CSD settlement.
  5. Asset-class tags: EQ equities, FI fixed income (rates and credit), FX, DR derivatives (OTC and listed); ALL when common to all four.
  6. external=true means usually run by a third party. The bank keeps oversight through C96 Third-Party and FMI Oversight, C51 Agent Bank Management and C95 Operational Resilience.
  7. Regulatory reporting is produced in S4 (transaction, derivative, transparency) and S8 (SFTR), and controlled centrally in P6 (C91). Surveillance and the control room sit in P6 as second-line functions.
  8. Cross-reference fields are left blank where no honest match exists. BIAN names are labels from v10, not structure; several are closest fit.
  9. FIX names are FIX Latest MsgType names; iso20022 lists business areas only, not message versions; swift_mt numbers are unverified analyst knowledge.
  10. Links are illustrative key flows in three threads; the real integration estate is far denser and most components read from the platform.
  11. No vendor product names and no statistics. Regimes marked not verified were not checked against the source for this draft and should be validated before external use.
  12. Office assignment is a convention; banks differ (for example some run prime services or clearing under Middle Office).

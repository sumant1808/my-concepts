The starting point: a bank that can take deposits, but only wholesale ones

WholesaleToday's deposit permission

The bank's UK subsidiary is a PRA-authorised bank. Its register entry limits accepting deposits to eligible counterparties and professional clients. Retail needs a variation to remove that limit, plus FSCS membership, retail conduct duties and a savings platform.

$6.4bnThe subsidiary's balance sheet

Mostly notes it issues and loans to group affiliates, with no customer deposits line. A £3-5bn retail book would be the same order of magnitude, so capital and liquidity need re-planning.

£5-50mPer platform, per month

Typical inflow targets for banks on a marketplace such as Raisin. Four platforms at about £30m a month would reach roughly £4bn in around 33 months (my arithmetic).

Why 1b is last

It pays the host's margin and also carries the costs of your own channel. You get neither the deposit nor the cheap channel. It only makes sense if the bank's existing client base is a very cheap source of savers, or the brand is the point.

Owning the vault wins at your target size

Option 2 overtakes the host-vault option 1a at about £2.1bn, so at £3-5bn it is clearly cheaper. The fully owned Option 3 passes 1a only at about £5.9bn, and never passes Option 2, because its own-channel acquisition cost (about 37bps) is higher than the marketplace fee (about 20bps).

What could change the ranking

Two sensitivities

Marketplace savers chase top rates, so that money may cost 10-20bps more than my base case. That adds about 15bps to 1a and 2: Option 2 would still lead at about 130bps. The host's cut is unpublished. Option 1a only matches Option 2 at £4bn if the host's margin were below about 37bps, against my 40-90bps estimate.

Liquidity upside (not in the figures)

Under the Basel liquidity coverage ratio, stable retail deposits run off at 5% (3% with qualifying insurance) and less stable at 10% or more, while short-dated funding from other financial institutions counts as 100%. Replacing £4bn of that with less stable retail frees about £3.6bn of liquidity buffer. At an assumed 20-50bps carry cost on the buffer, that is about £7-18m a year. It applies only to short-dated wholesale funding, and the PRA's own rules need checking.

FSCS levy and retail conduct

The FSCS levy for 2026/27 is forecast at £247m across all classes, with the deposits class shared in proportion to protected deposits, so it is small next to the funding at stake. I have not priced it. Consumer Duty, complaints handling and operational resilience would be new burdens for a wholesale-focused bank. I have not researched or priced these either.

Pros and cons at a glance

1a · Host vault + marketplace

Pros

  • Fastest and cheapest to launch (estimate 6-12 months)
  • Lightest regulatory load; the host carries licence, compliance and operations
  • The existing wholesale-only permission may already cover an onward placement from a host (my inference)

Cons

  • The deposit is the host's; funding comes only through an onward placement with a host margin
  • Two counterparties, and host large-exposure limits may cap the placement
  • Not evidenced for a third party's own product
1b · Host vault + your own channel

Pros

  • You own the customer relationship and brand
  • Host carries the licence and regulatory load
  • Proven pattern for brands (The AA, Saga on NatWest Boxed)

Cons

  • Highest running cost: host margin plus your own channel costs
  • The deposit is still the host's
  • Drifts towards a retail franchise, which is not the objective
2 · Your vault + marketplace

Pros

  • Lowest running cost at £3-5bn
  • The deposit and the funding are yours; rate and volume are yours to dial
  • Marketplaces onboard banks quickly (Raisin: about 6 weeks, no setup fee)

Cons

  • Needs a retail deposit permission, FSCS membership, conduct duties and a savings platform (est. 18-24 months)
  • Per-platform volume ceiling, so several channels needed
  • Rate-sensitive money; you do not own the customer
3 · Your vault + your own channel

Pros

  • Full control and the customer relationship
  • Highest long-run optionality; fixed costs spread over a larger book
  • Marcus-style proof of scale (about £21bn by mid-2020)

Cons

  • Highest one-off cost and slowest to launch
  • Own acquisition cost is above the marketplace fee on my estimates
  • Heavy retail brand and service load

Five checks before committing

  1. Meet the PRA before anything else

    Adding retail to a wholesale-only deposit permission is a substantial change to the business model. A pre-application meeting will tell you scope, evidence needed and a realistic timeline, which are my least certain estimates.

    Regulatory
  2. Source the bank's sterling funding spread

    The whole "cheaper funding" case turns on it. Replace the bracket above with the bank's internal figure.

    Economics
  3. Test the host cells in practice

    Ask ClearBank, Starling and NatWest Boxed whether a third party's own savings product can be held under their licence, at what margin, and whether they can place £3-5bn onward with one bank.

    Evidence
  4. Check intra-group use of retail funds

    The subsidiary already lends to group affiliates. Retail deposits funding affiliates would likely get closer PRA scrutiny; large-exposure and liquidity rules need checking.

    Structure
  5. Replace estimates with quotes

    Run cost, marketplace terms, host margin, own-channel acquisition cost and the one-off build are straw-man ranges until real quotes arrive.

Recommendation

Rent the front door.
Own the vault.

Option 2 is the cheapest to run at £3-5bn and keeps the funding the bank's own. It takes 18-24 months, so decide now whether to bridge through a host.

01Start the permission now.

Open a pre-application conversation with the PRA about removing the wholesale-only limit. It is the critical path for Options 2 and 3.

02Rent distribution, not a brand.

Plan for several marketplaces and a rate premium. Skip Option 1b unless the bank's own client base is a cheap channel.

03Treat the host as a bridge, not the answer.

Option 1a is faster but unevidenced and leaves the funding with the host. It wins only if speed and light regulation outrank owning the funding.

Appetite for AI in banking is real, but it falls fast as the machine takes control.

Acceptance falls as autonomy rises

An FCA survey of over five thousand UK adults asked how they would use AI for their banking.TA1

AI reviews and recommends (L1)TA136%
AI acts, with permission each time (L2)TA230%
AI acts alone within set rules (L3)TA320%
So what

L2 is the highest level that at least three in ten customers would use. That is where this bank sets its default.

67%

worry about being left unprotected if something goes wrong.TA7

The two things that would make people adopt: clear rules that protect them if it goes wrong (32%) and a proven record of accuracy (30%).TA12

Protection is the product.

Act II

The bank's assistant.

Bank-supplied agents only, in assist mode.

The bank supplies the assistant. The customer sets the permissions. A person owns every decision that can hurt.

Why only the bank supplies agents

Third-party agents

Customers bring their own agent to the bank.

  • Who bears the loss is unresolved between customer, developer, bank and merchant.TB7
  • Regulators say the frameworks were not built for autonomous agents.TB6
  • Banks must verify the authority of anyone acting for a customer.R9b
vs
Bank-supplied assistant

One assistant, owned by the bank, acting for the customer.

  • Firms stay responsible for consumer outcomes, including through AI.R1b
  • A named senior manager owns each AI use case from design.R7b
  • Redress and escalation sit with one accountable party.

The autonomy dial: assist mode by default

L0-L1Inform and suggest

Explains, shows, recommends. The customer acts alone.

DEFAULTL2Prepare, person approves

The assistant prepares; the customer or a colleague approves each action.

L3Act within limits

Opt-in only. Reversible, low-risk actions inside limits the customer set, notified each time.

Not offeredAutonomy that can harm

No decline, restriction, repricing, recommendation or complaint outcome by the assistant.

Credit and affordability decisions stay human. Automation is allowed, but the lender must show it works.R4fR5a

What the assistant never does, and always does

Never

Decisions with consequences stay with a person.

  • Decline or limit-cut a customer
  • Restrict or close an account, or reprice
  • Recommend a mortgage (that is advice)R5c
  • Decide a complaint or a reimbursement
  • Decide whether a customer is vulnerableR1b
Always

Principles built into every journey.

Customer promises
  • Says it is the bank's AI, in every channel
  • One tap to a person
  • Explains every decision, names the owner
  • Permissions page with an inform-only switch
  • Activity log the customer can download

Smarter spending: where the data and offers come from

The assistant reads the customer's spending, finds leaks and matches offers. Two routes bring in the data and the offers. Both need the customer's consent, and both leave the bank accountable.FS5FS7

Direct partnerships

The bank signs merchants itself.

  • Full control of terms, and who sees what
  • Clear line of accountability
  • Fewer merchants, slower to grow
Aggregators

Offer networks and open banking data providers.

  • Wide coverage fast, and a fuller view of other banks
  • One more party in the data chain, so due diligence and contracts matter
  • Account data from other banks must come from a regulated provider with explicit consentFS5

What stays human, by role

  1. Advisers and contact centre

    Mortgage recommendation and suitability, vulnerability determination and adjustments, complaint outcome, and the right to override the assistant.

    CD VUL ADV
  2. Underwriters and credit

    Every credit decision and decline, affordability judgement, policy exceptions, and confirming that decline reasons are true.

    CRED AFF ADM
  3. Fraud, KYC and financial crime

    Suspicious activity filing, freeze or exit decisions, sanctions true-match, scam-hold release and reimbursement rulings.

    AML APP
  4. Compliance, risk and senior managers

    Autonomy ceilings, model approval, Consumer Duty outcome sign-off, breach notification, and attestation that reasonable steps were taken.

    CD SMCR MRM

Each role keeps evidence of what the assistant showed and what the person decided. Senior managers must take reasonable steps and own risks from design stage.R7b

Four lines of control around the assistant

1stBusiness

Runs the assistant at L2, logs overrides, escalates incidents.

2ndRisk and compliance

Sets limits, challenges outcomes by customer group, oversees Duty and vulnerability.

3rdInternal audit

Independent assurance over both lines and the model lifecycle.

Act V

Built around
the rules.

What the journeys must satisfy, and what is not yet settled.

Six rules the journeys are built around

  1. Consumer Duty

    Act in good faith, avoid foreseeable harm, support customers' objectives, at every stage of the journey. No dark patterns.R10aR10c

    CD
  2. Automated decisions

    Solely automated significant decisions are allowed with safeguards: information, representations, human intervention, and the right to contest.F11

    ADM
  3. Credit and affordability

    Reasonable creditworthiness assessment for loans and cards; mortgage affordability gate; automation allowed if effective.R4aR4fR5a

    CRED AFF
  4. Advice boundary

    A personal recommendation of a mortgage triggers suitability. The customer chooses advised or non-advised, and a human adviser owns advice.R5cR5d

    ADV
  5. Vulnerability

    Four drivers: health, life events, resilience, capability. Detection by AI must be tested; vulnerable customers are not excluded from AI journeys.R6aR1b

    VUL
  6. Complaints and accountability

    Final response within eight weeks; Ombudsman wants a clear rationale for AI's contribution; a named senior manager per AI use case.R8aTB8R7b

    DISP SMCR

What is not yet settled

  • The FCA's review of the regulatory perimeter for AI agents is due roughly October 2026 to January 2027, and may add rules.TB4
  • Senior manager function numbers and some rule references are unverified and must be checked before a client sees them.
  • Detail of the 2025 mortgage advice changes comes from a secondary source.R5d
  • ICO guidance on automated decisions is still draft.F12
  • No audited UK evidence of underwriting productivity gains; what exists is a vendor case study and a target.TD7TD8

Lead with protection and accountability, not productivity.

The evidence for customer demand is about trust. The evidence for productivity is mostly colleague time saved.TD4TD2

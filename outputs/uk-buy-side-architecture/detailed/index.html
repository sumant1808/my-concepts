Limitations and honesty

What this diagram does not establish.

  • BIAN was checked against the version 10 service landscape only. It is oriented to banks and has no buy-side or private-markets coverage, so the layering is the analyst's own and BIAN names are labels, not structure. A card with no BIAN line had no matching domain.
  • UK rules are named as in the FCA Handbook. Those marked not verified were not checked against the source for this draft; validate before external use.
  • ISO 20022 business areas are verified. Message-level mappings (for example setr for fund orders, seev for proxy voting, camt for cash statements, acmt for accounts) come from analyst knowledge and need confirming.
  • The key flows are illustrative. The real integration estate is far denser, and most components also read from the platform layer.
  • This is an archetype, not a client estate. It assumes one manager acting as both UK UCITS manager and AIFM, and it leaves out hedge funds, synthetic ETFs and advice.
  • Status: draft analyst design, AI-reviewed, not practitioner-validated. It has no agentic AI layer, no vendor names and no market statistics.

Assumptions

Choices made to draw one archetype.

  1. 1Archetype: UK-authorised manager acting as authorised fund manager for UK UCITS/OEICs and ETFs, and as UK AIFM for closed-ended private funds; segregated institutional mandates also in scope.
  2. 2Fund accounting, NAV, transfer agency and private fund administration are often outsourced; components flagged external=true are commonly run by an administrator, TA, custodian or specialist agent, but the manager keeps oversight and data ownership through Delegate Oversight (C80-C82).
  3. 3Hedge funds, synthetic replication, wealth/advice and the manager's own HR/procurement are out of scope; derivatives appear only as overlay, hedging and collateral.
  4. 4Segment tags: A = Active long-only listed, P = Passive/ETF, L = Alternatives (PE, private credit, real assets); ALL = shared across all three. Components tagged A,P serve both listed businesses.
  5. 5BIAN names come from the publicly visible v10 value chain view; later releases may rename or add domains. BIAN is bank-centric, so mapped names are closest-fit, not exact equivalence; empty means no matching domain was seen.
  6. 6CALM 1.1 node-type and relationship vocabulary is used for legend labels only (actor, ecosystem, system, service, database, network, ldap, webclient, data-asset). This is not a CALM document: flows and controls are not modelled, and FIX and Swift are message standards, not CALM protocols.
  7. 7ISO 20022 entries cite verified business areas (setr, sese, semt, seev, colr, reda, auth, acmt via Swift doc); specific message-level mapping (e.g. setr for fund orders, seev for proxy voting, camt for cash statements) is analyst knowledge and should be confirmed.
  8. 8UK rules named but not verified in this run: Consumer Duty, SM&CR, UK UCITS/COLL, UK AIFM regime, UK MiFIR transaction reporting, FATCA/CRS, operational resilience, CASS and UK EMIR/SFTR. SDR, CCI (PS25/20) and Stewardship Code 2026 were verified.
  9. 9Functional system view only: no agentic AI layer, no vendor or product names, no market statistics.
  10. 10Links show representative flows only, not the full integration estate; most components also read from the Platform layer.
  11. 11IBOR sits in Investment Management (decision support); ABOR sits in Operations (official record). Many firms merge them or outsource ABOR to the administrator. Alternatives positions are held in C24-C26 and C51; a total-portfolio view comes from the data platform (C72).

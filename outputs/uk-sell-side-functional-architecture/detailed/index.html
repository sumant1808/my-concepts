Sell-side global markets: functional architecture from pre-trade to post-trade

Eight horizontal layers in five bands: lifecycle layers from pre-trade to post-trade, cross-lifecycle services that act on positions at several points, and common services that serve every layer above. Standards are inputs, not the structure.

Limitations and assumptions

What this diagram does not establish, and the choices made to draw one archetype.

Limitations

  • Layering not yet reviewed. The component content was AI-reviewed. The grouping of components into eight layers and five bands is an analyst judgement on an archetype and has not been practitioner-validated.
  • CALM is vocabulary only. Every component carries a CALM 1.1 node type as a label, but this is not a CALM document: no relationships, flows or controls are modelled.
  • BIAN names are closest-fit labels, checked against the version 10 service landscape only. BIAN is oriented to banks and has no lifecycle view of capital markets, so the structure is the analyst's own. A card with no BIAN line had no matching domain. Later releases may rename domains.
  • ISO 20022 is shown at business area level only (for example secl, sese, colr, auth). Message identifiers were not checked, and sell-side use of setr and fxtr was not checked.
  • Swift MT numbers are analyst knowledge and are not verified. Verified: the coexistence of ISO 15022 and ISO 20022 for securities (MT to ISO 20022 is coexistence, not a forced cut-over), and the end of MT/MX coexistence for cross-border payments on 22 November 2025 (MT103/202 retired).
  • 19 of 41 standards entries are marked not verified, including many regulatory regimes. They were not checked against the source for this draft and should be validated before external use: UK MiFIR, best execution, pre- and post-trade transparency, EMIR risk-mitigation and clearing duties, CFTC Part 43, SEC SBSR, UMR phase-in and CCP margin models, CASS and 15c3-3, Reg SHO, 15c3-5 and RTS 6, KYC and AML, MiFID categorisation and suitability, ISDA, GMRA and GMSLA, LEI, ISIN, CFI and FIGI, FRTB, SA-CCR and BCBS 239, ISDA DRR, US broker-dealer regimes, MiFID II recording of communications, UK MAR Articles 11 and 18, and the Swift MT numbers. Mapping to components is indicative, not legal or regulatory advice.
  • FIX names were checked against a third-party FIX Latest dictionary. CDM was checked at model level, not function level. FpML and CDM references are family level.
  • The key flows are illustrative; the real integration estate is far denser and most components also read from the common services.
  • Not every trade passes every lifecycle layer: FX skips custody, uncleared OTC skips the CCP, listed derivatives skip CSD settlement.

Assumptions

  1. Archetype: a global bank's markets division (sales and trading only) with UK, EU and US legal entities, dealing as principal and agent across EQ, FI, FX and DR.
  2. Out of scope: primary issuance (ECM/DCM), research production, corporate and retail banking, treasury ALM beyond trading funding, and agentic AI.
  3. The bank runs its own custody business (L5, owner stage S7), so custody components are internal; CSDs, ICSDs, CCPs, CLS, agent banks, trade repositories, triparty agents, data vendors and the messaging network are third-party run and appear as external parties. Four FMI touchpoints (C28, C41, C49, C50) are drawn inside their lifecycle layer (L4, L5) to show the flow; they are the same parties as X13, X04, X05 and X07.
  4. The architecture is a functional layer view, read top to bottom from pre-trade to post-trade: three lifecycle bands (B1 to B3, five layers), one cross-lifecycle band (B4, two layers) and one common services band (B5, one layer). The owner's 10-stage Trade Flow Value Chain is kept only as a traceability tag ('stages') on every layer and group. A trade does not pass every lifecycle layer: FX skips custody, OTC uncleared skips CCP clearing, listed derivatives skip CSD settlement.
  5. Asset-class tags: EQ equities, FI fixed income (rates and credit), FX, DR derivatives (OTC and listed); ALL when common to all four.
  6. external=true means usually run by a third party. The bank keeps oversight through C96 Third-Party and FMI Oversight, C51 Agent Bank Management and C95 Operational Resilience.
  7. Regulatory reporting is produced and controlled in one cross-lifecycle layer, L7: G13 holds transaction, derivative, transparency and SFTR reporting (C35, C36, C37, C66) with the reporting control hub (C91). Surveillance and the control room (P6) and conduct and client assets oversight (G27) sit in L7 as second-line functions.
  8. Cross-reference fields are left blank where no honest match exists. BIAN names are labels from v10, not structure; several are closest fit.
  9. FIX names are FIX Latest MsgType names; iso20022 lists business areas only, not message versions; swift_mt numbers are unverified analyst knowledge.
  10. Links are illustrative key flows in three threads; the real integration estate is far denser and most components read from the platform.
  11. No vendor product names and no statistics. Regimes marked not verified were not checked against the source for this draft and should be validated before external use.
  12. Office assignment is a convention; banks differ (for example some run prime services or clearing under Middle Office). Layer office is the dominant owner; each group keeps its own office from the stage view.
  13. Financing is not the last step of a trade. Securities lending, repo, prime margin lending and synthetic financing finance positions that already exist, from trade date to settlement and for as long as the position is held, and collateral, liquidity and cash serve margin (L4), settlement (L5) and financing alike. They form cross-lifecycle layer L6, not a stage after custody.
  14. Market data is not a step in the trade. Prices, curves and ticks are consumed by quoting (L2), execution and algos (L3), margin (L4), valuation and risk (L7) and surveillance (L7), so market data sits in common services (L8) beside reference data and is available to every layer.
  15. Risk, control and regulatory reporting are not a step between confirmation and clearing. Limits gate onboarding and pre-trade checks, reports are due at execution and on lifecycle events, and surveillance reads orders as well as trades, so they form cross-lifecycle layer L7.
  16. Reconciliation is a shared service (L8, G28): C99 reconciles front to back and against CCPs, clearing brokers, triparty agents and counterparties, and C53 reconciles nostros and depots, so both read from every lifecycle layer and from L6. OTC portfolio reconciliation (C30) stays in L4 because it is a bilateral confirmation duty with counterparties.
  17. The 'stages' tag on a group names where its content sits on the owner's current slides: S0 to S9 for the stage strip and PLATFORM for the cross-cutting platform strip. Each group also carries 'origin' (old stage or platform group).
  18. Slide fit: seven layer rows for the lifecycle and cross-lifecycle bands (L1 to L7), with common services (L8) drawn as one foundation strip beneath them, as the platform was before. L1 and L2 are kept apart, while L3 merges execution with trading and booking, L4 merges confirmation with clearing and L5 merges settlement with custody.
  19. Links, threads and component fields are unchanged; links gain from_layer and to_layer, externals gain layers, and standards gain the layers that use them. Verified and unverified flags are exactly as before.
